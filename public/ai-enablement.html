PROPOSED ENABLEMENT PLAYBOOK

A repeatable path from first use to lasting practice.

This is a proposed operating approach for a broader organization, not a claim that I have already run a company-wide AI program.

01 · CHOOSE THE WORK

Start with outcomes and roles

  • Identify meaningful, frequent workflows with the people who do them.
  • Set a clear baseline, intended benefit, risk level, and accountable owner.
  • Group learners by role, AI fluency, and the tasks they need to perform.
02 · LEARN IN CONTEXT

Teach through realistic practice

  • Build learning paths from everyday use to advanced building, matched to access and responsibility.
  • Use real tasks, worked examples, prompt and use-case libraries, and practice with feedback.
  • Show when to verify, escalate, or avoid using AI.
03 · SUPPORT THE CHANGE

Make help easy to find

  • Use staged rollouts, demonstrations, office hours, and a peer champion community.
  • Maintain a searchable knowledge hub with current guidance, FAQs, examples, and known limits.
  • Invite feedback and remove workflow, access, and confidence barriers.
04 · LEARN AND ADJUST

Measure capability and value

  • Review adoption, proficiency, workflow outcomes, user friction, and control signals together.
  • Compare results with an agreed baseline and segment by role or workflow.
  • Update learning and safeguards as tools, tasks, and evidence change.

ILLUSTRATIVE SCORECARD · PROPOSED, NOT HISTORICAL RESULTS

Measure whether people can use AI well.

I would agree on definitions and baselines with the organization before reporting impact. Usage counts alone would not establish proficiency or business value.

Reach & sustained use

Participation, repeat use, and coverage by role or workflow.

Demonstrated proficiency

Task-based checks for appropriate prompting, verification, and judgment.

Workflow outcomes

Agreed quality, completion-time, rework, or service measures compared with a baseline.

Trust & support

Feedback, help requests, review and escalation adherence, and reported failure patterns.

Operating cost

Model and token use, compute, licensing, and support costs considered alongside the workflow and its outcomes.

Time regained and reinvested

What people do with capacity freed from routine work, and what that makes possible for service, quality, problem-solving, or collaboration. Capacity is not automatically a financial saving.

Potential measures only; no outcome figures are claimed here. Select measures that fit the workflow, protect people and data, and can be interpreted responsibly.
