GOVERNANCE STARTER KIT

Make responsibility clear before AI changes the work.

A practical starting checklist for teams considering an AI-enabled workflow. Scale the review to the consequences, the people affected, and the organization’s existing controls.

01 · DEFINE THE USE

Establish purpose and ownership

  • Name the business owner and the people accountable for the system.
  • Describe the work being changed, who is affected, and the intended benefit.
  • Identify the data involved, its source, permitted use, and sensitivity.
  • Check applicable organizational policy, contractual duties, and legal review needs.
02 · ASSESS THE CONSEQUENCES

Decide where human authority belongs

  • Consider what could happen if the output is wrong, incomplete, biased, or unavailable.
  • Set the system’s allowed role: inform, recommend, prepare, or act.
  • Specify which decisions require a person’s review and approval before action.
  • Define when the system must defer, stop, or route an issue to an accountable person.
03 · VALIDATE BEFORE USE

Test against real operating conditions

  • Use representative examples, including difficult and unusual cases.
  • Check accuracy, consistency, access controls, data handling, and traceability.
  • Record limitations, unresolved risks, required human checks, and approval owners.
  • Agree on success measures and conditions that would pause or stop the work.
04 · OPERATE AND REVISIT

Keep ownership active after launch

  • Assign responsibility for monitoring, support, incidents, and user concerns.
  • Keep records sufficient to understand important outputs and decisions.
  • Provide a workable fallback when the system is unavailable or unreliable.
  • Review the controls when the model, data, workflow, or consequences change.

A starting point for discussion—not legal advice or a substitute for organization-specific security, privacy, compliance, and risk review.

OTHER LEADERSHIP MODELS

Ways I frame the work.

Business-first opportunity model

Choose work because it matters to people and the organization, not because a tool is available.

Human–AI work design

Shape the relationship between automation and human judgment around the consequences of the work.

Adoption as operating-model change

Consider how responsibilities, skills, and decisions shift when AI becomes part of everyday work.

Evidence-to-value model

Distinguish activity from meaningful improvement, and make uncertainty visible in the story.

Build, buy, partner, or defer

Keep multiple paths open, including the choice to wait until the organization is ready.
