01

My question

When an AI system summarizes a report, can I quickly tell which statements the source supports, which are interpretation, and which are still unknown? I want to make that check visible instead of treating a polished answer as proof.

This page is my working demonstration of that question: a public-source case, a repeatable review template, and an evaluation method I can apply to another topic. I’ll update it as I build and learn.

Everything here is independent study based on public material. No employer, client, workplace, or internal information is used. The sample is AI-assisted and unmeasured; it does not claim a deployment or outcome.

Public-company analysis

Duolingo, Inc. · NASDAQ: DUOL · As of November 24, 2026

What the company reported

  • Daily activity: average daily active users were 64.9 million in Q3 2026, versus 50.5 million in Q3 2025, a reported year-over-year increase of 29%.
  • Paid subscribers: 13.1 million at quarter end, versus 11.5 million a year earlier, a reported increase of 14%.
  • Revenue: $309.0 million in Q3 2026, versus $271.7 million in Q3 2025, a reported increase of 14%.
  • Speaking practice: management said the proportion of exercises learners could answer by speaking had doubled since the beginning of 2026. The letter described Video Call as an AI-powered feature and said expansion to all Super subscribers was planned by year-end.
  • Cost signal: management guidance expected Q4 2026 gross margin of 72%, down 90 basis points sequentially as AI-feature rollout expanded. This is management guidance, not a reported result.

All metrics and product statements above are attributed to company filings. They have not been independently audited in this exercise.

What this suggests — and what remains unknown

Interpretation

The public materials point to a product trade-off worth watching: richer speaking practice may create learner value, while broader AI use brings per-unit cost and margin questions. A useful evaluation would pair learning progress and repeat practice with cost and access measures. Daily activity alone does not establish that learners learned more.

The public rollout described in the letter is tied to Super subscribers. This review cannot tell whether the change improves learning outcomes, how usage differs across free and paid learners, or whether lower per-unit AI costs offset total feature costs.

Forward-looking details above remain management plans or guidance. This is an independent reading of public materials, not inside knowledge or a stock recommendation.

The review brief

A reusable source-review brief

I use a compact brief to make my reasoning inspectable: start with a bounded question, attach source and date to each material fact, then put interpretation and unknowns in their own sections. The Duolingo sample shows what that looks like; the blank template makes the method reusable.

Download the source-review template Download the completed sample brief
What the template asks the reviewer to check
  • Can every material fact be traced to a dated primary source?
  • Are reported results, estimates, company guidance, and interpretation kept separate?
  • Are contradictions, missing data, and failed checks visible?
  • Does the conclusion state what the public evidence cannot answer?
05

How I check it

A method I’d use for a small personal experiment; no comparative results yet.

Judge the answer against a manual reference

  1. Define the task. Choose one bounded question and a manual reference answer before using a model.
  2. Limit the material. Use public documents for this exercise. Record the source version and date; do not submit private or restricted data.
  3. Check every claim. Trace names, dates, figures, and citations back to the original page or filing. Keep reported results apart from guidance and inference.
  4. Measure the whole effort. Compare like-for-like runs, including prompt setup, verification, corrections, and any failed attempts. Track factual errors and citation coverage, not just speed.
  5. Keep a person accountable. A human checks and owns the final brief. Stop if a key claim is unsupported, citations fail, source rights are unclear, or the task cannot be reviewed safely.

This keeps quality, effort, and human review in the same frame. I have not yet run a controlled comparison or collected results.

What I’d build next

Proposed personal project · Not started · Not an employer onboarding plan

A source-checking research notebook

Days 1–30

Days 1–30 · Make a reference set

Choose one public question, collect a handful of primary sources, write a manual reference brief, and define what counts as supported, unsupported, or uncertain.

Days 31–60

Build and compare

Make a small notebook that stores each claim beside a source passage and locator. Compare AI-assisted briefs with the manual reference; log unsupported claims, missed citations, corrections, and total review time.

Days 61–90

Test the edges and publish

Try varied document types and deliberately ambiguous questions. Inspect where the workflow fails, state which checks still need a person, and publish the notebook with its sample data and limits. Decide whether to improve it or stop.

This is a proposal for personal study, not a completed project or employer plan. No deployment or measured result is claimed.
